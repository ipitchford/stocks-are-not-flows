# Public-data pilot preregistration

**Project:** Housing dissaving / concentration-to-tenure  
**Protocol:** England 2008–2019 accounting facts and structural identifiability gate  
**Version:** 0.1, frozen 1 August 2026  
**Status:** prospective analysis contract; not yet an OSF registration  
**Inputs:** official and primary-publisher sources in `DATA_SOURCE_LEDGER.csv`  
**Causal status:** non-causal accounting and structural-diagnostic pilot  

## 1. Purpose and relationship to the novelty target

This protocol operationalises the Stage 1 data-feasibility audit. It freezes the public-data accounting facts, calibration-target/holdout split, denominators, geography rules, measurement boundaries, falsification rules, and Gate D synthetic-recovery tests before the public pilot is run.

It does **not** identify a historical causal effect of top wealth concentration on owner occupation. It also does not silently amend the frozen novelty target in `outputs/novelty-target.yaml`, whose proposed structural endpoint comparison is 2006–08 to 2020–22 for English households with a reference person aged 25–44. This pilot instead uses a pre-pandemic **2008–2019** core because EHS begins in merged form in 2008 and WAS Round 8 has material COVID-era collection and methodology breaks [`EHS_EUL_SERIES`; `WAS_EUL_7215`]. If the final paper substitutes the pilot window for the frozen 2006–08 to 2020–22 estimand, that is a change of estimand and requires a novelty-gate amendment rather than an unreported drift.

The public pilot has two decisions:

1. **Gate A — accounting feasibility:** can official public/registered data reproduce their own benchmarks and yield coherent, separately labelled England, Great Britain, and UK moments?
2. **Gate D — structural identifiability:** can the proposed estimator recover a top non-housing saving-pressure shock separately from middle-resource, credit, supply, tax, rate, and demographic shocks in synthetic data generated by the model?

Only after both gates pass may the empirical team begin a structural calibration that reports a unique top-pressure contribution. A Gate D failure redirects the paper to identified sets, bounded scenarios, or comparative counterfactuals.

## 2. Existing-data disclosure

All datasets already exist, and public aggregate trends have necessarily been seen during source verification and the earlier novelty review. The supplied bundle may also contain exploratory calculations. This document is therefore an **analysis-plan preregistration for secondary data**, not a claim that no researcher has ever observed the data.

At the time this protocol is frozen:

- no new public-data accounting pipeline specified below has been executed;
- no restricted source has been requested or accessed as part of this Stage 1 continuation;
- target and holdout moments have not been extracted under the definitions below; and
- numerical thresholds in Gate D have not been tuned to empirical fit.

Any pre-existing result that exactly matches a registered moment will be labelled “previously observed” in the results ledger. Confirmatory and exploratory analyses will be separated.

## 3. Research questions and bounded predictions

### 3.1 Accounting questions

**RQ-A1.** How did owner occupation and private renting change from EHS 2008–09 to 2018–19 among English private households whose household reference person (HRP) was aged 25–44?

**RQ-A2.** How did the distribution of net non-housing wealth change across complete pre-pandemic WAS rounds in Great Britain, and is the measured change separable from property-price revaluation?

**RQ-A3.** What were the contemporaneous paths of England house prices, stock rents, housing supply, transaction turnover, and UK mortgage costs from 2008 through 2019, with each source retaining its own geography and denominator?

**RQ-A4.** Can a model with top pressure distinguish that primitive from middle impoverishment, credit, supply, landlord-tax, interest-rate, and demographic paths using the frozen target moments?

### 3.2 Directional predictions

These predictions test the historical narrative; they are not causal hypotheses.

- **P1:** the EHS owner-occupation share for HRP age 25–44 is lower in 2018–19 than in 2008–09.
- **P2:** the EHS private-renter share for HRP age 25–44 is higher in 2018–19 than in 2008–09.
- **P3:** the survey-weighted top-decile share of net non-housing wealth is higher in the last complete core WAS round than in the first.
- **P4:** the England HPI-to-PIPR index ratio is higher in December 2019 than in January 2015.
- **P5:** on the model branch claimed to represent top pressure, an increase in the primitive top non-housing resource/saving-pressure shock has the joint signature: owner occupation falls; landlord-held housing rises; house prices rise; and the model's rental yield falls.

If P1–P4 fail or their uncertainty intervals include no directional evidence, the relevant historical premise is “not supported in the registered window”; it will not be rescued by changing endpoints. If P5 fails on the claimed stable branch, the mechanism-integration claim stops regardless of empirical fit.

## 4. Design and assurance boundary

### 4.1 Design

This is a secondary-data, observational accounting exercise followed by model-based synthetic recovery. There is no randomisation, treatment assignment, or blinded outcome coding. Public administrative time series and weighted repeated cross-sectional/longitudinal survey summaries are aligned for calibration, not joined at the person or property level.

### 4.2 Claims permitted

The accounting stage may use “measured,” “changed,” “is higher/lower,” and “co-moves.” Structural results may use “within the model,” “conditional on the maintained assumptions,” and “model-implied contribution.”

The following language is prohibited on the basis of this pilot:

- “top wealth caused the decline in home ownership”;
- “owners sold to landlords”;
- “cash buyers are landlords”;
- “PPD Category B identifies buy-to-let”; or
- “the price-rent index is landlords' net yield.”

### 4.3 No restricted-data substitution

Only sources marked `open_*` or `registered_eul` in `DATA_SOURCE_LEDGER.csv` may enter this pilot. Account-licensed, secure, unresolved, member-only, or paid data remain dependencies and cannot be silently substituted. Published aggregates from a mixed-access publisher may be used only when the exact table is publicly downloadable and its reuse terms are recorded.

## 5. Geography, time, units, and denominators

### 5.1 Geography invariant

Every result label begins with one of `England`, `England and Wales`, `Great Britain`, or `United Kingdom`.

- **England** is the core housing population: EHS, England cuts of UK HPI/PIPR, England-restricted PPD, and MHCLG housing stock/supply.
- **Great Britain** is the WAS wealth population. A GB wealth moment may discipline a UK/England structural model but is never described as an England observation unless an approved file supports a defensible England restriction.
- **United Kingdom** mortgage and HMRC aggregates are macro-financial/contextual moments. They are not rescaled into England observations.
- **England and Wales** HMLR ownership files remain separately labelled and are outside the public pilot when account licensing is required.

No pooled regression may mix these populations as if they were observations from a common sampling frame.

### 5.2 Time invariant

The core stops before pandemic-era data:

- Monthly administrative series: **1 January 2008 through 31 December 2019**.
- Calendar-year summaries: **2008 through 2019**, using all 12 months; a year with fewer than 12 valid monthly observations is incomplete and cannot be a primary target.
- EHS: **2008–09 through 2018–19**, where the label is the survey/financial year beginning in the first named year. The 2019–20 wave is excluded from the confirmatory core because it crosses into 2020. It may enter a sensitivity analysis only if interview dates allow a clean end at 31 December 2019.
- MHCLG annual financial-year series: **2008–09 through 2018–19** for direct alignment with the EHS core. A 2019–20 value is sensitivity only.
- WAS: complete **Wave 2, Wave 3, Wave 4, Wave 5, and Round 6** observations are the primary wealth sequence. Round 7 spans 2018–20 and is sensitivity only if the licensed data permit a pre-1 January 2020 interview restriction; otherwise it is excluded. Round 8 and later are outside the core.

Survey field periods are never collapsed to a sharp 1 January date. Charts display the full field-period label.

## 6. Frozen variable construction

### 6.1 Tenure

Primary owner occupation uses the harmonised EHS categories `own outright` plus `buying with a mortgage or loan`. Shared ownership is excluded from the primary numerator and reported separately. A sensitivity series adds shared ownership only if the EHS documentation confirms a stable category over all core waves. Private renting comprises positive-rent households renting from a private landlord, letting agency, employer, or private individual/relative under the harmonised EHS tenure variable; rent-free households remain separate. Social renting remains a third category.

The analysis will publish a round-by-round category crosswalk before computing trends. If a category cannot be harmonised without outcome-informed judgment, the affected wave is excluded and Gate A records the failure.

### 6.2 Net non-housing wealth and top group

For household (i) in WAS round (t), define:

\[
NHW_{it}=\text{net financial wealth}_{it}+\text{net business wealth}_{it}.
\]

The primary measure excludes main and other property wealth, all housing-secured debt already represented in net property wealth, physical wealth, and private-pension wealth. Source-provided imputations are retained and flagged. Negative net financial/business values are retained; primary estimates are not winsorised.

The top group is the survey-weighted top decile of `NHW` among `D_WAS_ALL` within each complete round. The weighted threshold is calculated from that round alone. The primary top-share statistic is:

\[
S^{NHW}_{10,t}=\frac{\sum_i w_{it}NHW_{it}1\{NHW_{it}\ge q^{w}_{0.90,t}\}}
{\sum_i w_{it}NHW_{it}}.
\]

The statistic is invalid if the weighted denominator is non-positive. Ties at the 90th percentile are included and the resulting weighted population share is reported. Top 1% estimates are exploratory only because WAS top-tail nonresponse and sample size make them unsuitable as a primary target [`WAS_EUL_7215`].

For longitudinal sensitivity, wealth rank is frozen at the first observed pre-period round. Households are not re-ranked using post-period property prices. A change in wealth stock is never labelled saving without a documented holdings/valuation decomposition.

### 6.3 Prices

Use the published **England, all-property-types** UK HPI index [`HMLR_UKHPI`]. Monthly index values are retained as published. Annual level is the arithmetic mean of the 12 monthly index values. Annual growth is (100[\log(HPI_{Dec,t})-\log(HPI_{Dec,t-1})]). The endpoint price change is (100\log(HPI_{Dec2019}/HPI_{Jan2008})). Recent-period revisions are irrelevant to the historical cutoff only after the archived vintage and checksum are recorded.

FTB/former-owner and cash/mortgage HPI cuts are holdouts. Neither cut identifies landlords.

### 6.4 Rents and the yield boundary

The primary public rent level is the survey-weighted median weekly cash rent among `D_EHS_PRS_RENT`, reported in nominal pounds for each EHS year. No real-rent growth is computed until an official price deflator has been separately verified and added to the ledger.

ONS PIPR/IPHRP are **stock-rent indices**, not new-let or asking-rent series [`ONS_PIPR`; `ONS_IPHRP_HIST`]. The public pilot will:

1. report 2008–2014 historical IPHRP growth, if the exact series passes the method-field check, as a separately labelled pre-break segment;
2. report January 2015–December 2019 PIPR growth as the post-break segment; and
3. never level-splice the two segments across December 2014/January 2015.

For January 2015–December 2019 only, normalise both England all-property HPI and England PIPR to 1 in January 2015 and define:

\[
PRI_t=\frac{HPI_t/HPI_{Jan2015}}{PIPR_t/PIPR_{Jan2015}}, \qquad
SRP_t=PRI_t^{-1}.
\]

`PRI` is a **house-price-to-stock-rent index ratio** and `SRP` is its inverse. Neither is a level of gross rental yield, still less net yield. They omit rent and price level alignment, voids, repairs, management costs, taxes, leverage, transaction costs, and capital gains. The empirical paper may compare the direction of `SRP` with the model's yield, but it may not calibrate a net-yield level to `SRP`.

A model net-yield target is prohibited until a separately verified dataset and formula cover, at minimum, rent level, property-value level, voids, operating/management costs, taxes, and the exact leverage convention. Adding such a target requires a protocol amendment.

### 6.5 Transactions and company ownership

Primary PPD turnover uses Category A standard residential transactions completed in England during each calendar year, divided by England dwelling stock at 31 March of that year and multiplied by 1,000 [`HMLR_PPD`; `MHCLG_DWELLING_STOCK`]. All-category turnover is a sensitivity result. Category B is never treated as buy-to-let.

CCOD and OCOD do not enter this public pilot because they require an account/licence and omit private individual landlords [`HMLR_CCOD`; `HMLR_OCOD`]. If later authorised, company-title additions form a separately bounded corporate-ownership outcome, not a general landlord-acquisition measure.

### 6.6 Supply

For financial year (t/t+1), define the England net-additions rate:

\[
NA_t=100\times\frac{\text{net additional dwellings during }t/t+1}
{\text{dwelling stock at 31 March }t}.
\]

Use official MHCLG England totals [`MHCLG_NET_SUPPLY`; `MHCLG_DWELLING_STOCK`]. It is a flow into the dwelling stock, not a planning-permission, construction-start, quality-adjusted supply, or local supply-elasticity measure.

### 6.7 Mortgage costs and credit

Use monthly Bank of England quoted rates `IUMBV34` (two-year fixed owner-occupier, 75% LTV) and `IUMZID4` (two-year fixed buy-to-let, 75% LTV) on their common non-missing support [`BOE_OWNER_2Y75_IUMBV34`; `BOE_BTL_2Y75_IUMZID4`]. Define:

\[
Spread^{BTL-OO}_t=IUMZID4_t-IUMBV34_t.
\]

If common support within the core contains fewer than 36 consecutive months, the spread moves from target to exploratory context and the target/holdout split is amended before calibration. Bank Rate is a contextual policy path; FCA MLAR aggregate lending is a holdout [`BOE_BANK_RATE_IUDBEDR`; `FCA_MLAR`]. Quoted rates are offers for specified products, not realised average borrower costs.

## 9. Extraction, exclusions, missingness, and uncertainty

### 9.1 Extraction freeze

For each source, record URL, exact filename, publisher version/edition, download UTC timestamp, SHA-256, licence, coverage, geography, and whether values are provisional. Raw files are immutable. All tables are regenerated from scripts.

### 9.2 Survey rules

- Use source-provided final cross-sectional weights for cross-sectional moments and longitudinal weights only for explicitly longitudinal sensitivity analyses.
- Use source-provided imputed values in the primary analysis and retain imputation flags.
- Exclude only observations outside the registered population or lacking a variable required for that particular moment. Report pre/post-exclusion weighted and unweighted counts.
- Do not trim or winsorise primary wealth estimates. Sensitivities use 0.5/99.5 and 1/99 weighted-percentile winsorisation and are labelled non-primary.
- Suppress a subgroup moment from target/holdout scoring if its unweighted denominator is below 100. It may be shown descriptively with an instability warning only if licence/disclosure rules permit.
- If more than 10% of the weighted eligible denominator lacks a required non-imputed field, the primary moment fails Gate A unless an official derived/imputed variable resolves it.
- Where strata and PSU fields are available, use Taylor-linearised design-based standard errors. If the access tier withholds them, use the official replicate/variance method if provided; otherwise label weight-only intervals approximate.

### 9.3 Administrative-series rules

- Preserve published seasonal-adjustment status. Do not mix adjusted and unadjusted series.
- Do not interpolate missing primary monthly values. An annual target requires all 12 months.
- Use HMLR PPD Category A as primary; report Category A+B only as sensitivity.
- Freeze the last historical data vintage before computation. If a source revises the 2008–19 history later, rerun a separately versioned revision sensitivity rather than overwriting results.

### 9.4 Inference

Accounting facts are reported as estimates, endpoint differences, and 95% uncertainty intervals. P-values are not the primary decision device. If formal directional tests are included, they are one-sided only for P1–P4 and Holm-adjusted across those four predictions; two-sided estimates and confidence intervals remain the main display. No unregistered covariate search or subgroup search may upgrade an unsupported primary prediction.

## 10. Gate A and falsification rules

### 10.1 Benchmark reproduction

Before original calculations, reproduce at least one official published benchmark for each microdata family.

- Weighted shares must agree within the larger of 0.5 percentage points or the official table's rounding unit.
- Weighted means/totals must agree within the larger of 2% relative error or the official rounding unit.
- Medians/quantiles must use the source's documented weighted-quantile convention; any remaining discrepancy is documented rather than silently re-coded.

An unresolved failure closes Gate A for that dataset. The source may remain contextual but cannot generate calibration targets.

### 10.2 Accounting identities

- Mutually exclusive EHS tenure shares under a common denominator sum to 100% within 0.1 percentage point after rounding.
- PPD annual counts equal the sum of included monthly Category A counts exactly.
- Annual HPI/rate averages equal the arithmetic mean of the 12 archived monthly observations to machine precision.
- Wealth component sums reproduce the registered `NHW` construction to machine precision after source missing codes are converted.
- Geography labels and periods in every output must match the registry; a mismatched label is a blocking error.

### 10.3 Known-negative and mutation controls

The pipeline must reject or visibly relabel each of these deliberate invalid constructions:

1. adding property wealth to `NHW` and calling the result an exogenous top-pressure measure;
2. re-ranking top households using post-period total wealth;
3. calling PPD Category B a buy-to-let flag;
4. splicing IPHRP and PIPR levels across January 2015 without a break indicator;
5. calling `SRP` a net yield;
6. combining EHS household shares with dwelling-stock denominators;
7. relabelling UK mortgage aggregates as England observations; and
8. including Round 8 WAS in the pre-pandemic core.

A control that passes silently is a code-review blocker.

### 10.4 Historical-narrative falsification

The registered historical narrative is unsupported in the public window if any of the following occurs:

- P1 or P2 has the opposite sign; an interval spanning zero is inconclusive rather than supportive;
- P3 has the opposite sign or `NHW` cannot be harmonised without property wealth;
- the full model misses the sign of both `H01_TENURE_MIDPATH` and `H05_PRICE_RENT_INDEX` blocks; or
- the no-top-pressure model's primary holdout weighted RMSE is no more than 10% worse than the full model's score in **each** of the three holdout blocks.

The last rule means the top-pressure mechanism must improve holdout RMSE by more than 10% in every block to support a broad incremental-explanatory claim. Failure does not invalidate the theoretical comparative static; it blocks the claim that the extra mechanism materially explains the registered UK facts.

## 11. Gate D: synthetic recovery and identifiability

Gate D is run **before unsealing empirical holdouts**. Its purpose is to establish that the estimator can distinguish mechanisms when the model is known to be true.

### 11.1 Frozen shock blocks

The estimator must separately represent these seven path/amplitude blocks:

\[
q=(\Delta z_T,\Delta z_M,\Delta c,\Delta H,\Delta \tau_L,\Delta r,\Delta d),
\]

where `z_T` is primitive top non-housing saving pressure; `z_M` is middle resources; `c` is the mortgage/credit wedge; `H` is housing supply; `tau_L` is landlord tax/transaction wedge; `r` is the interest-rate path; and `d` is demographics. Nuisance structural parameters are estimated or fixed according to a parameter-to-moment map frozen before this test.

The executable model commit, equations, parameter bounds, transformations, target vector, moment scaling, and random seeds must be hash-pinned in a future `GATE_D_MANIFEST`. Bounds may be altered only before any empirical holdout is unsealed and with a dated rationale.

### 11.2 Standardisation and local sensitivity

Map every estimated parameter to a unit interval using its frozen lower/upper bounds. Scale each target moment by its registered standard error; where unavailable, use the section 8.2 fallback.

At baseline and at 64 stratified points in the admissible parameter region:

1. compute the central finite-difference Jacobian of scaled target moments with respect to scaled parameters using a step of 1% of each parameter's range;
2. repeat at 0.5% and 2% steps; and
3. calculate singular values, numerical rank, and condition number for the focal seven shock-block columns and for the full estimated parameter matrix.

**Local-identification pass:** the focal shock-block Jacobian has full column rank at at least 61 of 64 points; its smallest/largest singular-value ratio is at least `1e-4` (condition number at most `1e4`); and rank is invariant across the three step sizes at at least 61 points. Failure at the empirically selected solution is automatically blocking even if the 61/64 aggregate rule passes.

### 11.3 Synthetic design

Generate truth cases in normalised parameter space:

- 256 deterministic maximin Latin-hypercube points within `[0.10, 0.90]` on every estimated dimension;
- one zero-shock anchor plus positive and negative one-at-a-time anchors for each of the seven shock blocks (15 anchors total); and
- two opposite-sign cases for each of the 21 shock-block pairs (42 pairwise-confounding cases).

Total: **313 truth cases**. Each case is simulated once without measurement noise and 20 times with independent mean-zero Gaussian moment noise using the registered target covariance/scaling, giving 6,573 fits. Use 32 deterministic space-filling starting values per fit; retain every start and optimiser code/status.

The noise model is a recovery stress test, not a claim that all empirical moment errors are Gaussian.

### 11.4 Recovery criteria

All errors below use normalised parameter-range units.

1. **Noiseless recovery:** at least 95% of truth cases have total parameter RMSE no greater than 0.02, focal `Delta z_T` absolute error no greater than 0.05, and normalised target-moment RMSE no greater than `1e-6`.
2. **Noisy focal recovery:** median absolute error for `Delta z_T` no greater than 0.10; 90th-percentile absolute error no greater than 0.25.
3. **Sign recovery:** where the true absolute `Delta z_T` is at least 0.20, its estimated sign is correct in at least 90% of noisy fits.
4. **Null false positive:** in zero-`Delta z_T` cases with rival shocks active, no more than 5% of nominal 95% profile intervals exclude zero.
5. **Shock classification:** the largest true shock block is correctly classified from the unrestricted estimate in at least 90% of one-at-a-time and pairwise cases; top pressure versus middle resources must separately reach 90%.
6. **Optimiser stability:** at least 95% of cases have 30 of 32 starts converge to the same objective basin within `1e-6` relative objective value.

### 11.5 Profile and rival-compensation tests

For 64 preselected truth cases spanning the design, profile `Delta z_T` on a fixed 41-point grid over its full admissible range while re-optimising all rivals.

- The profile must have one connected 95% set using the one-parameter objective-difference cutoff of 3.841 under the registered normalised quadratic objective.
- The connected set's width must be no more than 50% of the preregistered `Delta z_T` range in at least 90% of the 64 cases.
- Fix `Delta z_T` at least 0.25 range units away from truth and re-optimise all rivals. If the resulting target-vector root-mean-square distance is at most one registered standard error in more than 10% of cases, top pressure is deemed observationally substitutable and unique attribution fails.
- Repeat the compensation test specifically against `Delta z_M`, `Delta c`, and `Delta H`; each pair must pass independently.

### 11.6 Structural known-negative controls

Run these model mutations on the same synthetic harness:

- set `Delta z_T=0` while rival shocks are non-zero;
- switch off the wealth/saving-pressure channel if the model has such a control;
- set the housing-supply response to its fixed-stock boundary;
- set the landlord-tax wedge to zero; and
- swap the labels of owner and landlord housing outputs.

The estimator must not recover a positive top shock from the first two controls, and the output-identity tests must catch the label swap. The claimed top-pressure branch must reproduce P5 under the unmutated model and fail or change in the theoretically predicted way under the relevant mutation.

### 11.7 Gate D decision

**PASS-UNIQUE** requires every local-sensitivity, recovery, classification, profile, compensation, optimiser, and known-negative criterion above. There is no discretionary averaging across failed tests.

Any failure yields **FAIL-UNIQUE**. The permitted next steps are:

1. report an identified set/profile rather than a point historical contribution;
2. report scenario-conditional top and middle decompositions;
3. simplify the parameterisation based only on synthetic diagnostics and re-register before unsealing holdouts; or
4. stop the structural attribution claim.

Adding empirical moments after seeing holdout fit to force Gate D through is prohibited.

## 12. Model comparison and reporting

For target fit, report the normalised quadratic objective by moment block, raw residuals in natural units, and parameter uncertainty. For holdouts, report raw predictions, errors, weighted RMSE by the three blocks in section 8.3, and the full-versus-no-top-pressure comparison. Do not aggregate away a failed block.

Required model variants are:

1. full model;
2. no top-pressure path;
3. no middle-resource change;
4. fixed credit path;
5. fixed supply path;
6. fixed landlord-tax path; and
7. combinations needed for the registered two-order Shapley decomposition.

The Shapley result remains a structural decomposition. The two orderings, level contributions in percentage points, contribution relative to observed tenure change, and residual/interactions must all be reported. If the pilot window replaces the frozen endpoint window, the novelty target must first be amended.

## 13. Stopping, amendments, and exploratory work

The public accounting pilot stops after `AF01`–`AF14`, Gate A, and Gate D are complete. It does not continue until a preferred narrative is obtained.

Permitted amendments before any target extraction must be dated, justified, and preserve the prior version. After target extraction, amendments may fix demonstrable coding/source errors but both original and corrected results are reported. After holdouts are unsealed, changes to parameters, targets, model equations, or fit weights create a new exploratory specification.

Exploratory work includes subregions, top 1%, alternative age bands, Round 7 partial-period estimates, Round 8+, inheritance/lottery/pension mechanisms, HRAD event studies, corporate ownership, local PPD regressions, alternative rent bridges, and net-yield construction. None may replace a failed registered result without a new prospective protocol.

### MVP hard stops

Stop the MVP or narrow its claim if any of the following occurs:

- EHS or WAS benchmark reproduction fails at the registered tolerance;
- core tenure or `NHW` categories cannot be harmonised without outcome-informed recoding;
- a necessary target requires a restricted source or a geography relabel;
- fewer than 36 common monthly observations exist for the registered BTL-owner quoted-rate spread and no prospective target amendment is made;
- rent construction requires a level splice across the 2015 method break or is described as net yield;
- property wealth, post-period ranks, PPD Category B, cash purchases, or corporate ownership are used as an investor-treatment proxy;
- a holdout is used in fitting without re-registration;
- any known-negative mutation passes silently; or
- Gate D returns `FAIL-UNIQUE` while the paper continues to report a unique historical top-pressure contribution.
